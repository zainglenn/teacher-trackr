# HOD Action Plan — English Middle & High School (Grades 5–9)
## Dubai Schools Al Khawaneej | Academic Year 2026–2027
**Candidate: Zain Glenn**

---

## Vision

To build a cohesive, standards-driven English department where every student in Grades 5–9 is challenged by a well-sequenced curriculum, every teacher is growing professionally, and every decision is grounded in evidence — not assumption.

My approach in the first year is to listen before leading, audit before changing, and build systems that make excellent practice the default rather than the exception.

---

## Context

Dubai Schools Al Khawaneej is a public-private partnership school following the American Curriculum, anchored in NYSED ELA standards. The Middle and High School English department (Grades 5–9) spans five year groups, each taught in parallel classes with a shared long-term plan reviewed and approved by the HOD.

Grade 9 sits at a critical transition point. Students entering Grade 10 must be well-prepared for the increased demands of upper secondary ELA. This year group will receive close attention as a priority.

## Strategic Priorities

### Priority 1 — Curriculum Coherence Across All Five Grade Levels
Every class in every year group must have a complete, standards-aligned Long Term Plan reviewed and approved before teaching begins. Curriculum consistency is not aspirational — it is a minimum standard.

### Priority 2 — Differentiated Teacher Development
Teachers at different stages of their career need different support. Experienced teachers need challenge and leadership opportunity; developing teachers need structured guidance and specific, documented feedback. Everyone receives coaching that is calibrated to where they are — not a one-size programme.

### Priority 3 — Data-Informed Decisions
Standards coverage, student attainment, and planning quality are tracked consistently across all year groups. Decisions about intervention, resource allocation, and professional development are grounded in evidence collected throughout the year — not surfaced in a June report.

### Priority 4 — Transition Readiness at Grade 9
Students leaving Grade 9 are prepared to access the demands of upper secondary English. Planning, assessment design, and teaching at this level must reflect that expectation explicitly.

## Term-by-Term Action Plan

---

### Pre-Year Preparation (June – August 2026)

**Know the Department Before Arriving**

- Request and review all existing Long Term Plans from 2025–2026 across Grades 5–9. Look for: consistency in standards coverage, quality of essential questions, evidence of standard-to-assessment alignment.
- Review any available student attainment data from the previous year. Identify year groups or classes with notable gaps before the new cohort arrives.
- Meet each teacher individually, ideally before the year begins. The goal is to understand their professional history, their relationship with planning documentation, and what they feel is working or not working in the department. Listen more than talk.

**Establish the Planning Framework**

- Publish the full-year planning calendar in Week 1 of Term 1. Every teacher knows every submission deadline before the year begins — no mid-term surprises.

| Milestone | Deadline |
|---|---|
| T1 Long Term Plans submitted | 7 Sep 2026 (Week 2) |
| T1 Long Term Plans reviewed by HOD | 14 Sep 2026 (Week 3) |
| T2 Long Term Plans submitted | 30 Nov 2026 (end of T1) |
| T2 Long Term Plans reviewed | 11 Jan 2027 (Week 1 of T2) |
| T3 Long Term Plans submitted | 10 Mar 2027 (end of T2) |
| T3 Long Term Plans reviewed | 7 Apr 2027 (Week 1 of T3) |

- Confirm the standard sets in use across all five year groups, including whether Grade 9 uses NYSED Grade 9–10 standards or an alternative framework. Resolve this with SLT in the first week.

---

### Term 1 — Establish (24 Aug – 6 Dec 2026)

**Weeks 1–2: Foundation**

- Welcome the department. Share the planning calendar, the expectations for plan quality, and the review process. Be explicit: every unit plan is reviewed before it is taught, and feedback will be specific and standard-referenced.
- Begin getting into classrooms informally. Walk the corridors, drop in briefly, be present. The HOD who only appears in formal observations is not yet a colleague.

**Week 3: First Review Cycle**

- All Term 1 plans submitted. Review every unit plan against three criteria:
  1. The essential question creates genuine intellectual inquiry — it is not a topic heading.
  2. Selected standards are explicitly linked to the assessment task, not just listed.
  3. The plan covers the right standards for this unit in the year's sequence.
- Approve, request revision, or reject with written feedback. Every decision documented. Verbal-only feedback is not feedback — it is a conversation that will be forgotten.
- Target: every Term 1 unit approved before teaching begins.

**Weeks 3–6: Classroom Observation Round 1**

- Observe every teacher at least once. Focus question: does what is happening in the classroom match the approved plan, and do students know what they are working on?
- Post-observation conversation within 48 hours. Specific, forward-looking, standard-referenced. No general praise, no general criticism.

**Mid-Term Break (12–18 Oct): T2 Planning Window**

- Teachers begin drafting Term 2 units during the break. HOD available for planning conversations — not mandatory check-ins, but an open door.

**Weeks 7–13: Coverage Monitoring**

- Conduct a mid-term standards audit for each year group. Which standards are at risk of being missed entirely by Term 3? Flag this to individual teachers in a coaching conversation, not an email.
- Run a department meeting focused on one shared professional question — something drawn from what observations have surfaced, not an agenda item generated in advance.

**End of Term 1: Report to SLT**

- Deliver a one-page summary to SLT: where each year group stands in terms of coverage, what has been approved and what has required revision, and what the professional development focus will be in Term 2.
- Celebrate publicly what the department has done well. Address what hasn't worked privately.

---

### Term 2 — Develop (4 Jan – 14 Mar 2027)

**Week 1: T2 Review Cycle**

- All Term 2 plans were submitted before winter break. Review and approve in the first week back. Any plan not submitted before the break is a conversation, not a deadline extension — find out what got in the way.

**Weeks 2–5: Attainment Data**

- By Week 5 of Term 2, each teacher should have formal assessment data for at least two standards taught in Term 1.
- Look for patterns across classes in the same year group. If multiple teachers are seeing similar attainment dips on the same standard, that is a department-level issue — it belongs in a professional learning conversation, not a private note to each teacher.

**Professional Learning Focus: Assessment Design**

- Based on Term 1 observations and plan reviews, run a department-wide PLT session on assessment design: specifically, how does the assessment task generate evidence that the target standard has been met — not just completed?
- Use a real example (anonymised if needed) of a strong and a weak assessment task from within the department. Concrete is always better than abstract.

**Weeks 6–8: Observation Round 2**

- Second observation for every teacher. Compare to Term 1: has the plan-to-classroom alignment improved? Is student language more precise and standard-aware?
- Teachers who are growing should hear that specifically. Teachers who are not progressing need a direct, supportive conversation with a clear development focus and a timeline.

**Mid-Term Break (9–13 Feb): T3 Planning + Mid-Year Review**

- Term 3 plans drafted and submitted before spring break.
- Compile a mid-year coverage report across all five grade levels. Where are the gaps? What needs to change in T3 planning to address them?

**End of Term 2: Mid-Year Report to SLT**

- Present to SLT: standards coverage position by year group, student attainment patterns, summary of teacher development progress, and anything requiring leadership-level support.
- Flag any teacher who is significantly behind in planning or whose students show persistent attainment gaps. Agree a support structure with SLT where needed — this is not a unilateral HOD decision.

---

### Term 3 — Consolidate (29 Mar – 2 Jul 2027)

**Week 1: T3 Review Cycle**

- Term 3 plans reviewed and approved before the mid-term break. No class enters T3 without an approved plan.

**Weeks 2–6: Standards Completion**

- Cross-reference coverage across all year groups. Which standards are at risk of being untaught by end of year? Work with individual teachers to adjust remaining unit plans — redistribute, consolidate, or revisit where the sequence allows.
- No year group should close the year below 85% standards coverage. If any class is tracking below this by Week 6, the teacher and HOD co-plan a solution together.

**Grade 9 — Transition Readiness Review**

- By Week 6, conduct a specific review of Grade 9: are students demonstrating the level of analytical writing, extended reading, and independent inquiry that Grade 10 will demand?
- If not, work with the Grade 9 teacher to build a focused final unit or assessment that explicitly targets the transition gap.

**Weeks 4–8: Individual Development Reviews**

- Formal one-to-one with every teacher. Review the full year together: what did they plan well, where did they struggle, what do they want to develop next year?
- Use concrete data from their plans and attainment records. Make the conversation about evidence, not impressions.

**Mid-Term Break (24–30 May): Year-End Preparation**

- Begin curriculum audit for 2027–2028.
- Identify standards undertaught consistently across multiple classes — these become the starting point for next year's professional learning focus.
- Draft the 2027–2028 planning calendar.

**Weeks 9–13: End-of-Year Assessment**

- Final assessment data collected. Analyse by year group and by strand: which standards were mastered, which need reteaching at the start of next year?
- Flag students whose attainment trajectory suggests early intervention in Year 2 — pass this to the incoming class teacher before the year ends.

**End of Year: Department Report**

- Present to SLT: full-year coverage data, student attainment outcomes by year group and strand, teacher development summary, curriculum gaps identified, and recommendations for 2027–2028.
- All approved Long Term Plans archived as the baseline for next year's planning.

## Teacher Development Framework

Every teacher is placed in one of three development tiers based on their track record, experience, and the evidence of Term 1 observations and plan quality. This is a working model, not a permanent label — teachers move between tiers as they grow.

| Tier | Profile | HOD Support |
|---|---|---|
| **Leading** | Experienced, plans submitted early, strong standard-to-task alignment | Autonomy, leadership opportunities (e.g. lead a PLT session, mentor a colleague), stretch challenges |
| **Developing** | Capable but inconsistent — may struggle with specific areas (e.g. essential questions, assessment design) | Structured check-ins twice per term, targeted feedback, co-planning support on specific units |
| **Supported** | New to school, returning after a gap, or showing consistent planning gaps | Weekly contact, HOD reviews every unit before submission, co-plans at least one unit per term |

**No teacher stays in the Supported tier by design.** The goal is to move every teacher to Developing and every Developing teacher toward Leading.

## Curriculum Technology

To support the systems described in this plan, I have built a web-based curriculum tracker specifically designed for this school context — live at [curriculum-tracker-five.vercel.app](https://curriculum-tracker-five.vercel.app).

The platform gives the HOD a real-time view of standards coverage across all classes and year groups, manages the Long Term Plan submission and approval workflow, and records all HOD feedback against individual unit plans. Teachers build and submit plans through the same system; the HOD approves, requests revision, or rejects with written rationale — all documented and time-stamped.

It is not a replacement for professional judgement. It is infrastructure that makes good practice visible and consistent. The standards coverage heatmap, for example, turns what is usually a termly guesswork conversation into a factual one: which teachers are below 40% mapped standards in a given strand, and what are we going to do about it.

I designed it around the Grade 6 ELA standard set already in use at DSK. Extending it to cover all five year groups (Grades 5–9) is a pre-year task I have already planned for.

I mention it not to lead with technology, but because building it required me to think through every HOD workflow in this plan — the review process, the feedback loop, the coverage audit — and make each one concrete and repeatable. That is the same thinking behind this action plan.

## What I Will Not Do

- Wait until June to find out a year group has significant coverage gaps.
- Give general feedback. If a plan needs work, I will name the specific standard, the specific task, and the specific problem.
- Treat all teachers the same. An experienced teacher who is performing well deserves challenge and autonomy, not the same checklist as a teacher in their first year at DSK.
- Approve plans verbally. Every approval and every revision request is written down. This protects the teacher as much as it protects the process.
- Change everything in Year 1. Sustainable improvement comes from understanding what already works, building on it, and changing what genuinely needs to change — not from arriving with a fixed agenda.
